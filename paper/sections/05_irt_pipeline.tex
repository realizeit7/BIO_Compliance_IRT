\section{The Calibration Pipeline}
\label{sec:pipeline}

The frozen bank is produced by a two-phase pipeline that reads the
generated items, administers them to a grid of synthetic examinees, fits
Rasch, and applies item-fit quality control.

\subsection{Phase 1: the examinee grid}
Phase~1 builds a $3 \times 5 \times 3 = 45$-cell grid of virtual
examinees: three base models (\texttt{llama-3.3-70b-versatile},
\texttt{openai/gpt-oss-20b}, \texttt{llama-3.1-8b-instant}), five sampling
temperatures ($0.1, 0.4, 0.7, 1.0, 1.2$), and three system-prompt
strictness levels (\texttt{none}, \texttt{neutral}, \texttt{strict}).
Every (examinee~$\times$~item) pair is administered once and graded,
yielding a binary response matrix. On \todo{N\_in} items this is
\todo{$\approx$82{,}035} graded attempts. Each cell is a single
zero-shot call; the run is resumable and records one audit row per pair.

\subsection{The dual-mode judge}
Responses are graded by an LLM-as-judge in one of two modes. The
\emph{strict} judge (hard and real items) requires the correct
compliant/non-compliant conclusion \emph{and} a section-level citation
(e.g.\ ``21~CFR~11.10(e)''). The \emph{lenient} judge (easy items)
requires only the correct conclusion and consistent reasoning, no
citation. Without the lenient mode, easy items would be failed by weak
examinees that never cite sections, collapsing the low-difficulty anchor.
All judge errors are mapped to a failing score so that no exception
escapes the matrix.

\subsection{Rasch fit and baseline anchoring}
We fit a 1PL Rasch model by marginal maximum likelihood
(\texttt{girth.rasch\_mml}), fixing discrimination $a = 1$ for all items.
Because difficulty is identified only up to an additive constant, we
\emph{anchor} the scale: in the 1PL model
$P(\text{correct}\mid\theta,b)=\sigma(\theta-b)$, so shifting every $b$ by
a constant $c$ shifts every $\theta$ by $-c$. We choose
$c = \hat{\theta}_{\text{base}}$, the MLE ability of a designated baseline
examinee (\texttt{llama-3.1-8b-instant}, $t{=}0.1$, strictness
\texttt{none}), so that the baseline sits at $\theta = 0$ by construction
and all reported $b$ are on that scale.

\subsection{Phase 2: classical item-fit quality control}
Phase~2 refits Rasch on the Phase~1 matrix and drops items failing any of
four classical checks:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em,topsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Zero variance}: all-pass or all-fail items carry no
  information and have an undefined difficulty at the bound---dropped
  unconditionally.
  \item \textbf{Point-biserial} (corrected, item vs.\ rest-of-total):
  a non-positive correlation means stronger examinees do \emph{worse},
  signalling a broken gold standard---dropped at $\le 0$.
  \item \textbf{Infit} (information-weighted mean-square residual):
  dropped above $1.5$.
  \item \textbf{Outfit} (unweighted mean-square residual): dropped above
  $2.0$.
\end{enumerate}
The fit thresholds are deliberately \emph{asymmetric}, with upper bounds
only. Linacre's standard symmetric $[0.5, 1.5]$ band is calibrated for
thousands of examinees; on a 45-cell grid, an item with a very low
mean-square is a \emph{clean separator} we want to keep, not a defect.
Outfit gets the looser bound ($2.0$ vs.\ infit's $1.5$) because, being
unweighted, a single anomalous cell at extreme $\theta$ inflates it
disproportionately on a small grid.

\paragraph{Why Rasch and not 2PL.}
A 2PL model is not identifiable on a 45-examinee grid: marginal-MLE
pinned every discrimination at the optimizer's upper bound, and
joint-MLE---though it gives a real spread---is statistically inconsistent
as the number of items grows with examinees fixed, which is exactly our
regime. Rasch is identifiable, gives the specific objectivity we rely on
for the $\Delta\theta$ comparisons in Section~\ref{sec:deltatheta}, and
matches the model used during generation. The 2PL machinery is retained
for a future re-evaluation once the examinee count grows.
