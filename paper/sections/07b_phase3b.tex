\section{Architectural Interventions and Agent-Type \texorpdfstring{$\Delta\theta$}{Delta-theta}}
\label{sec:phase3b}

\subsection{Motivation}
Section~\ref{sec:deltatheta} established that system-prompt strictness
primarily unlocks citation \emph{format} rather than compliance
\emph{reasoning}, and that only the largest model showed a genuine
reasoning gain. A natural follow-up question is whether \emph{structural}
changes to the agent do better than prompt edits. So in this section I
test three architectural interventions, namely knowledge retrieval,
self-critique, and step decomposition, using the same frozen
$1{,}284$-item bank as the measuring instrument.

\subsection{Agent variants}
I implement three agent architectures. Each one is a drop-in replacement
for the zero-shot baseline in the Arena runner.

\paragraph{RetrievalAgent.}
Before the single LLM call, the agent embeds the item's context and
question with the \texttt{all-MiniLM-L6-v2} sentence-transformer model
(22\,MB, local, no API call) and retrieves the top $k = 3$ CFR sections
by cosine similarity from a pre-built numpy index. The index covers CFR
Parts 11, 50, 58, and 211 of Title 21, and Part 46 of Title 45. The
retrieved sections are prepended to the system prompt as a
\emph{Relevant Regulations} block. All other parameters are identical to
the zero-shot baseline, including strictness level, model, and
temperature. This isolates the effect of in-context regulatory grounding
without changing the agent's reasoning process.

\paragraph{CriticAgent.}
The agent produces an initial zero-shot answer, then issues a critique
prompt (``Review your previous answer for citation accuracy and
compliance determination. Correct any errors.''), and finally a synthesis
step. With one critique round this costs three Groq calls per item, which
is the default. All turns share the same conversation thread, so the
model has access to its own reasoning history.

\paragraph{StepDecompositionAgent.}
The agent breaks the compliance analysis into four sequential Groq calls,
each building on the prior step's output. The steps are to identify the
regulated activity and parties, to identify the governing CFR or ICH
provision, to assess compliance or violation, and to state the final
determination with a section-level citation. This mirrors a structured
legal-analysis workflow, and it tests whether explicit chain-of-thought
scaffolding improves compliance reasoning.

\subsection{Experimental grid}
The grid crosses two models (\texttt{llama-3.3-70b-versatile} and
\texttt{llama-3.1-8b-instant}), one temperature ($0.4$), two strictness
levels (\texttt{none} and \texttt{strict}), and four agent types
(\texttt{zero\_shot}, \texttt{retrieval}, \texttt{critic}, and
\texttt{step\_decomp}), which gives 16 examinee cells. Every cell is
administered the full frozen $1{,}284$-item bank and anchored to the
Phase~1 baseline ($\theta = 0$) using the same re-anchoring procedure as
Section~\ref{sec:deltatheta}.

\subsection{Interpretation}
The architectural interventions behave very differently depending on
whether the judge requires a citation. Under lenient scoring
(strictness=none) all three agents raise the 70b's $\theta$: retrieval by
$+1.07$, critic by $+3.45$, and step decomposition by $+4.55$ logits.
Under strict scoring the picture inverts, and all three \emph{lower} the
70b's $\theta$ (retrieval $-0.30$, critic $-0.94$, step decomposition
$-0.51$).

I read the lenient-side gains with some caution. The lenient judge
removes the citation requirement, but it does not isolate reasoning
cleanly, because it still rewards a well-organized answer that reaches
the right conclusion. So the honest statement is that these
interventions improve whatever the lenient judge measures, which is
conclusion accuracy together with the coherence of the supporting
argument. Calling that ``reasoning'' is a reasonable shorthand, but it is
not something this design can establish on its own.

The strict-side reversal is easier to interpret. When section-level
citations are required, citation format becomes the binding constraint.
The three interventions are built to improve the analysis rather than to
satisfy a citation format, and the extra structure appears to crowd out
the citation. A step-decomposition chain that reasons well through four
turns still fails if the final turn omits the section number.

The 8b model shows a weaker effect throughout. Retrieval gains $+0.35$
logits under lenient scoring, which is modest, and critic and step
decomposition give minimal or negative returns under both conditions. A
plausible reading is that the 8b has less capacity to make use of the
architectural support, or to compose a multi-step analysis into a
coherent citation.

Taken together, this section corroborates the central finding of
Section~\ref{sec:deltatheta}. When the judge enforces section-level
citations, format compliance dominates the measurement scale, and it does
so even for a model as large as the 70b.
