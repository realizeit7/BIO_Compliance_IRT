% Abstract. Covers the instrument, the two intervention experiments, and
% the validation work.
Evaluating large language models (LLMs) in a regulated domain such as
biopharmaceutical compliance requires more than an aggregate accuracy
score. A stakeholder needs to know how hard each question is, and where a
given system sits on a difficulty scale. In this paper I build an
automated pipeline that constructs a difficulty-calibrated question bank
for biopharma regulatory knowledge (FDA 21~CFR~Part~11, GCP, GMP,
promotional review, and informed consent) and calibrates it with item
response theory (IRT). The pipeline has four steps. First, it generates
compliance scenarios under a ``Red Herring plus subtle violation''
mandate that forces genuine difficulty. Second, it administers each item
to a population of synthetic AI examinees that spans models, sampling
temperatures, and system-prompt strictness. Third, it grades the
responses with a dual-mode LLM judge. Fourth, it fits a Rasch (1PL)
model, anchored to a fixed baseline examinee, and applies classical
item-fit quality control. Starting from 1{,}823 candidate items I freeze
a healthy bank of \textbf{1{,}284} items whose calibrated difficulties
span roughly $\pm 4$ logits. Items taken from real FDA warning letters
are harder than the synthetic items, although the difference is a shift
in the lower tail rather than a full separation. I then treat the
calibrated ability $\theta$ as an optimization metric and measure two
kinds of intervention: system-prompt strictness and agent architecture.
Grading the same responses with a strict judge and a lenient judge lets
me separate the part of each effect that comes from citation format from
the part that comes from compliance reasoning. For most models and most
interventions the effect is almost entirely format. Finally, I audit the
judge itself against two independent evaluation frameworks, and I
describe the expert-review protocol that is now in the field. Throughout,
I am explicit about the limits of a closed loop in which the same model
family generates, answers, and grades the items.
