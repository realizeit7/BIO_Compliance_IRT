\section{External Validation: Expert Review Protocol}
\label{sec:expertreview}

The audit in Section~\ref{sec:judgeaudit} compares rubrics that share a
model substrate. Breaking out of that loop requires a judgment the loop
cannot produce: a human regulatory professional's. We describe the
protocol here, in advance of its results, so that the instrument's
external-validation design is on the record and not contingent on the
outcome. Labels are being collected at the time of writing; we report the
sampling frame, the measurement plan, and --- importantly --- what a
negative result would look like.

\subsection{Sampling frame}
We drew a stratified sample of $50$ items from the frozen bank, ten from
each of the five hard domains. Items were restricted to those passing a
tightened quality filter (point-biserial $\geq 0.3$, infit $\leq 1.3$,
outfit $\leq 1.5$) and to the interquintile difficulty band
$b \in [0.83, 2.43]$ (the $20$th--$80$th percentiles of the healthy
bank), with a fixed random seed for reproducibility. The intent is to
audit items that the pipeline itself considers well-behaved and
typical: if the gold standards fail here, they fail everywhere, and a
sample drawn from the extremes would conflate item pathology with
authoring error.

One property of the sample is worth stating plainly, because it is a
limitation of the generator rather than of the sampling. The verdict
balance is $38$ \textsc{violation} to $12$ \textsc{compliant}. A balanced
split was not achievable: the ``Red Herring $+$ subtle violation''
mandate of Section~\ref{sec:itemgen} makes hard items
violation-bearing by construction, and only $16$ compliant-verdict items
exist in the entire quality-filtered hard-domain pool. This is a design
lesson for bank regeneration --- a difficulty mandate that always plants
a violation teaches an examinee that the answer is always
``violation'' --- and we flag it as such rather than presenting the
sample as balanced.

\subsection{Two-step measurement}
Each reviewer works through the items in a fixed order and, for each,
performs two operations whose order is load-bearing:

\begin{enumerate}[nosep,leftmargin=1.6em]
  \item \textbf{Independent verdict.} Reading only the scenario and the
        question --- with the reference answer withheld --- the reviewer
        records \textsc{violation}, \textsc{compliant}, or
        \textsc{ambiguous}.
  \item \textbf{Gold-standard grade.} The reference answer is then
        revealed and marked correct or incorrect, where correct requires
        both the right conclusion and a correct section-level citation.
        A written justification is required for every negative mark.
\end{enumerate}

Withholding the reference answer during step~1 is what makes the two
measurements independent. Reversing the order would anchor the reviewer
to the AI's conclusion and inflate agreement. Reviewers are asked not to
consult AI tools, and \textsc{ambiguous} is offered as a first-class
response: an item that two qualified professionals cannot resolve is a
defective item, and recording that is more useful than forcing a call.

\subsection{What the labels will estimate}
With $\geq 2$ independent reviewers the protocol yields three quantities
the present work cannot otherwise obtain:

\begin{itemize}[nosep,leftmargin=1.4em]
  \item \textbf{Gold-standard error rate.} The proportion of reference
        answers judged incorrect --- a direct estimate of how much
        authoring error the closed loop has injected into the bank, and
        the single number most needed to interpret every $b$ we report.
  \item \textbf{Inter-rater agreement} (Cohen's $\kappa$ on step-1
        verdicts). This calibrates the ceiling: if experts disagree with
        each other on these scenarios, no automated judge can be expected
        to do better, and the difficulty scale is measuring genuine
        regulatory ambiguity as well as model ability.
  \item \textbf{Human--machine concordance.} Agreement between expert
        verdicts and the pipeline's, per domain, indicating which domains
        need regeneration rather than recalibration.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Pre-committing to the failure modes}
Two outcomes would materially damage the claims in this paper, and we
state them now. First, a gold-standard error rate above roughly $10\%$
would mean the difficulty estimates are contaminated at a level that
recalibration cannot repair; the affected domains would require
regeneration and re-calibration, not adjustment. Second, low inter-rater
agreement ($\kappa < 0.4$) would indicate the items are ambiguous rather
than hard --- that the bank measures resolution of under-specified
scenarios rather than regulatory knowledge. Either finding would be
reported; neither is anticipated by anything in the current results, and
neither is excluded by them.
