The pipeline, the frozen bank, and every analysis output referenced in
this paper are available at
\url{https://github.com/realizeit7/BIO_Compliance_IRT} under the MIT
licence. The frozen bank
(\texttt{evaluator/output/phase2\_frozen\_bank.jsonl}, 1{,}284 items with
$b$, point-biserial, infit, and outfit), the $\Delta\theta$ tables for
Sections~\ref{sec:deltatheta} and~\ref{sec:phase3b}, and the judge-audit
scores and report are committed to the repository, so every number in
this paper can be recomputed without an API key. The two analyses that
are pure post-processing, namely the real-versus-synthetic test
(\texttt{scripts/real\_vs\_synthetic\_test.py}) and the figure scripts
(\texttt{paper/figures/gen\_fig*.py}), run offline from those files.

Two things are \emph{not} in the repository. The raw arena logs
(\texttt{logs/arena\_runs/*/responses.jsonl}), which hold one row per
examinee-item pair, are excluded for size. Regenerating them requires
re-running the pipeline against the Groq API, which is also what the
\texttt{py-irt} posterior standard deviations discussed in
Section~\ref{sec:discussion} are waiting on. The CFR embedding index used
by the retrieval agent is likewise rebuilt locally by
\texttt{scripts/build\_cfr\_index.py} from public eCFR sources.

\endgroup

\section*{Intended use}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Intended use}

This bank is a measurement instrument for evaluating language models. It
is not regulatory guidance, and it should not be used as one. The gold
standards were authored by a language model and, at the time of writing,
have not been validated by a human regulatory professional
(Section~\ref{sec:expertreview}). Section~\ref{sec:discussion} explains
why an error rate in those reference answers is plausible rather than
hypothetical. No item in this bank should be relied on to determine the
compliance status of any real system, process, or submission, and a score
on this instrument says nothing about whether a model is fit to be used
in a GxP setting.
